## The night before

- [ ] Consistency audit done; CV version they hold is correct (or a correction sent)
- [ ] Introduction rehearsed twice, under 90 seconds
- [ ] Mock drill scored; zeros redone
- [ ] Company brief and three questions ready
- [ ] Pinned repositories chosen (below)

## Pin the right repositories

Interviewers have opened candidates' repositories before calls. Pin 4–6 that show the role's skills, each with a README that states status and gaps honestly. For private repositories, offer a screen-share walkthrough. Only pin work you can explain line by line and whose provenance is clear (credit upstream projects).

## On the day

- [ ] Confirm the meeting link or office address from the invite; if onsite, arrive 15 minutes early
- [ ] Camera, mic, connection, a hotspot backup; quiet room; water; pen and paper for diagrams
- [ ] Tabs open: CV, portfolio, GitHub, the job description, live demos; a blank editor for coding
- [ ] Logged in 10 minutes early

## In the room

Think aloud; state assumptions; start simple, then add reliability, cost and security; end every design answer with what breaks first; stop talking when the answer is complete. If you don't know, say so and reason from principles ([12.2](/12-career-and-interviews/02-your-narrative)).

## After

- **Within an hour,** write down every question you were asked and how you answered.
- Mark each answer 0/1/2 using the [mock-drill scale](/12-career-and-interviews/05-technical-interview-playbook).
- Add new questions to your bank; fix weak answers; update this guide if a lesson was missing something.
- Send a short thank-you if it's customary where you are.

## Check yourself

<details markdown="1"><summary>What's the single most valuable thing to do after any interview?</summary>

Write down every question within an hour and score your answers; that list is the best preparation material for the next interview.

</details>
